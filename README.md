This meta-analysis aims to comprehensively evaluate the impact of different types of dietary fibers on the gut microbiome across diverse studies.


Practically, the aim of this thesis is to address four main biological questions:
1. How do different dietary fiber interventions modulate the gut microbiome in terms of both bacterial
composition and functional capacity?
2. Is it possible to perform a meta-analysis harmonizing both 16S and shotgun metagenomics sequencing data
across different types of dietary fiber interventions?
3. Can the baseline composition of an individual’s gut microbiome be used to accurately predict their response
to specific dietary fiber interventions, enabling personalized nutrition strategies?
4. Can dietary fiber supplementation improve the overall health of the gut microbiome?


To test these hypotheses, a standardized computational pipeline was applied to each dataset, allowing a consistent comparison across studies. The first part of the analysis focused on taxonomic changes, exploring how the relative abundance of bacterial taxa varied before and after each intervention.

Subsequently, functional alterations were evaluated by investigating changes in CAZyme repertoires and by evaluating changes in metabolic pathway activity through KEGG Orthology metabolic pathways. These differences were quantified using a linear mixed model and effect sizes were calculated as generalized fold change (gFC) value for every taxon, CAZyme family or other feature. This approach aimed to uncover how distinct DF types influence not only the microbiome composition but also its metabolic functionality potential.

In addition, a supervised machine learning model was developed to explore the bifidogenic potential of a microbiome when supplemented with dietary fibers. This was done by assessing whether Bifidobacterium relative abundance baseline could predict an individual’s response (defined as an increase/decrease in Bifidobacterium relative abundance) to supplementation.

Finally, an unpublished dysbiosis/eubyosis classifier, developed within our research group, was employed to identify those bacteria frequently associated with disease and health status, and use this information to further evaluate the beneficial impact of dietary fibers on gut microbial ecology.
